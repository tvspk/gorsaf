Build me an interactive app (artifact) for language practice. It should be a game I come back to because I want to, not a school exercise.

ABOUT ME
- I'm learning: [language, variety if relevant], level roughly [level], course/textbook: [name]
- Native language: [language]. Talk to me in it, briefly. The game's interface and narration are in [English / my language]; all exercises are in the target language.

MATERIAL AND HOW IT ARRIVES
- I'll give you textbook chapters (and their worksheets/homework) one at a time over the course of this conversation. Each new chapter is added to the same app as new cases next to the old ones; never replace or rebuild what is there, and never lose my saved progress.
- If I send several chapters at once, still build them chapter by chapter in order, with the full check after each one, and tell me where you are.
- Stick to the material. If there isn't enough, write your own sentences at the same level, but don't introduce new grammar.
- Never reuse sentences or answers from homework/worksheets; practise the same material with different sentences. Examples from the textbook itself are fine. Before adding new sentences, also make sure they don't duplicate sentences already in the game.
- The last chapter may be different (e.g. exam preparation). Treat it on its own terms: decide what fits best and explain why.

WORLD AND TONE
- Setting: [default: a completely ordinary municipal help desk on an orbital space station]. Everyday situations (bureaucracy, shopping, neighbours, work) against a fantastical backdrop. The vocabulary stays practical; only the circumstances are fantastical.
- Tone: [default: dry, ironic humour. The narrator is mildly weary of the world and occasionally pokes fun at me and at themselves. No cute mascots, praise kept sparse.]
- Characters and places recur across cases so the world holds together.

HOME SCREEN
- Top: course name and a small, quiet progress chip ("X/Y cases closed") in the same font as the rest.
- A row of numbered buttons (one per chapter + exam) to jump straight to a chapter, and a "Continue" button that opens the next unfinished required case.
- Chapters are collapsible groups; only one is open at a time (the current one by default). The collapsed header shows completed-case dots, "filed X% · cards Y%".
- Inside each chapter: a "Grammar notes" toggle with a short summary of that chapter's grammar, the cases, the vocabulary game and the flashcard drawer.
- An "Any time" section: Today's review, Overtime, and the [language-specific] trainer (see below).
- Everything that is a reward/gimmick rather than learning (station, maintenance, cabin, shop) sits in a separate, collapsible "bonus" section at the bottom, visually set apart (dashed divider with a label and a short subtitle such as "where the credits go"). Its open/closed state is remembered.
- At the very bottom, in small print: the save status, "Reset progress" (with confirmation), "Back up" and "Restore".

STRUCTURE OF A CHAPTER
- 3 cases per chapter (rounds of 5–10 minutes): 1) recognition, 2) actively writing full sentences, 3) optional, "a bit of everything" from the chapter. Case 1 is open from the start, case 2 after case 1, the optional one after case 1; the optional case doesn't block progress and gives a small commendation pin instead of a souvenir.
- A case has four phases (with a phase bar shown only inside cases):
  1. Story and mystery: short scenes where I answer in the target language (multiple choice, fill in the word, writing sentences). Ends with a reveal or punchline and a souvenir that appears in my cabin.
  2. Puzzle: calm logic tasks built on the language (work out a rule from examples, build a sentence from tiles with distractor tiles, match pairs, find the odd one out).
  3. Speed round: 60 seconds, multiple choice, combo multiplier for a streak (×2 after 3, ×3 after 6, ×4 after 10), personal best. Questions are generated from tables of forms, not a fixed list, and only cover the chapter being played. After a mistake the timer pauses and a short explanation is shown. Within one round nothing I answer correctly comes back; a mistake comes back once, a few questions later, marked "back again". If I exhaust all different questions before time runs out, the round ends early with "counter cleared" and a bonus point per second left.
  4. Maintenance (see Bonus).
- At the end of a case: a summary by skill (what I'm good at, what to review with a one-sentence rule) and an offer of the next case or Overtime.

EXERCISE DETAILS
- Every question belongs to a named skill (tag). Whatever I get wrong comes back later in a different sentence or task type, also in the next case.
- Immediate feedback after every answer; for a mistake, a one- to two-sentence explanation of why.
- Written answers: accept multiple correct variants; tolerate minor typos and missing diacritics unless they are the point of the exercise ([e.g. the start of the word for mutations, the verb ending]), which must be exact. Under every text field, in small print: "Accents (…) are optional." Distinguish "right words, wrong [mutation/ending]" from a completely wrong answer.
- When a question is shown outside its story (review, Overtime, maintenance inspections, returning items), show a small "Topic: … · Chapter N" line above it. Any prompt whose answer depends on tense/form must state the tense/form explicitly ("short future, type the verb"), so it is never guesswork.
- Sentence-building tiles: tap moves a tile between tray and sentence; tiles can also be dragged with a finger to reorder them or drop them at a specific place (works with mouse and touch).
- Matching: the second group (e.g. sentence endings) has a subtly different background tint so the two groups are distinguishable when stacked on mobile.
- Never ask anything from a chapter I haven't played: Overtime, review, stray vocabulary and inspections only draw on chapters with at least one closed case. Clean up any stale weak spots from unplayed chapters on load.

VOCABULARY GAME ("lost property")
- Include every word from the chapter's word list, no exceptions. Question types: meaning (target → mine), the reverse, [plural], [gender / another property with grammatical consequences], and writing the word from memory.
- Every word must come up before any word repeats.
- A word is "filed" after correct answers in two different question types, "gold" once I write it correctly. Home shows "filed X%" per chapter.
- If you're unsure about a word's gender or form, don't ask about it and tell me which words they are.

FLASHCARD DRAWER (per chapter, open regardless of progress)
- All words of the chapter plus useful phrases; both directions, mixed; sessions of 15; three buttons: "no idea" (comes back in the same session), "later" (to the end), "I know".
- A coloured stripe by word type: [red feminine noun, blue masculine noun, green adjective, grey verb, purple other/phrases], with a legend on the first card. No pictograms. Back side shows the article/mutated form and plural where relevant. Regional variants are marked with a small badge (e.g. "N." for northern forms).
- Example sentences on the back for words and short phrases (not for phrases that are already sentences): a short sentence in the target language with the word highlighted (also in its mutated/inflected form), and the translation hidden behind a small "▸ translation" tap. Store them separately from the cards so they can be fixed or switched off.

TODAY'S REVIEW (spaced repetition, in "Any time")
- Cards I know and skills I get wrong are scheduled at 1, 3, 7, 14, 30, 60 days; a miss resets to tomorrow. One round = up to 10 grammar questions + cards, max 30 items; "another round" if more are due. The button shows how many are due or when the next review is.

[LANGUAGE-SPECIFIC] TRAINER
- [Example for Welsh: "mutation trainer" — 60-second rounds drilling only the triggers of finished chapters, with modes all/soft/nasal/aspirate and its own record.] Only material from fully finished chapters.

DAILY STREAK
- A small visual row for the last seven days (today on the right) with weekday abbreviations in the target language; a day counts after [5] correct answers. Milestones (3, 7, 14, 30, 50, 100 days) give bonus credits. On mobile the row sits full-width above its text.

EXAM / FINAL CHAPTER (if the course ends with one)
- A timed mock exam that mirrors the real exam's written parts (e.g. article with questions, emails, gap fill, news items, letter), no hints and no marking until the end, alternating papers on resits, a certificate in the cabin on passing. Plus vocabulary game and flashcards for that chapter.

BONUS (rewards, separated and collapsible)
- Credits are earned only by correct answers (not by time spent).
- Maintenance: parts of the [station] can be repaired; each repair is a "work order" of three questions (two right to pass), paid only on success. Each part changes the rules (longer speed round, forgiven mistakes, second attempts, more credits, bonus questions from weak spots, crossed-out wrong options…). Calibrate for the whole course: each part has 3 tiers with rising price, tiers 2–3 unlock after later chapters; new parts appear as chapters are finished; finishing a chapter can break one working part (wear), which must be re-repaired. A map shows the state of every part.
- Cabin: souvenirs from cases and pins from optional cases, plus a shop of purely cosmetic items for credits shown on a second wall.

TECH
- Everything in one file; distinctive design with the atmosphere of the world (e.g. dark station, target-language sentences on "official paper slips", rubber stamps on answers), animations, works well on mobile and desktop (no horizontal scroll).
- Save progress to my account (artifact storage) so it works across devices and survives updates; the game must also run without saving. Version the save format and migrate old saves (renamed words keep their progress; new fields get sensible defaults). Browser storage only for tiny conveniences (e.g. which section is collapsed).
- Backup/restore: a copyable code (version + checksum) containing the whole save; restore checks the code, shows a summary (date, cases, credits) and replaces progress only after confirmation.
- Keep all language content (vocabulary, tables, stories, puzzles, review pool, notes, flashcard examples) in one clearly organised data block, separate from game logic.

WORKFLOW AND CHECKS
- For each chapter: read the textbook chapter and homework, build the 3 cases, notes, vocabulary, flashcards (+ examples), speed-round tables, review items; then check: every target-language sentence correct and natural (follow the textbook's variety and patterns); no overlap with homework or existing game sentences; every word of the list present; questions unambiguous out of context; play it through on mobile width without console errors.
- Publish updates to the same artifact URL.
- Afterwards tell me, briefly and in my language, what you added, what you verified and what you didn't, and what you're unsure about in the language. Say clearly that the content is not native-checked.
- If something essential is missing, ask; otherwise build. After each chapter, wait for my "ok" before the next one, unless I said to continue without stopping.
